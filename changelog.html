1457360d2026-08-27 23:27

Draft day: live injury status, and a bench with a shape

Two unrelated jobs, both aimed at Sunday.

The one that matters: the agent now re-pulls injury status every ten minutes
during the draft and prints what changed at startup. The board freezes
injury_status when it is built and sleeper_read caches the player dump for 24
hours, so a knee blown out on Saturday night was invisible at a Sunday 3pm
draft -- and unlike every other failure in this system, that one cannot be
undone afterwards. Live status overrides the board's frozen copy, Doubtful now
counts as undraftable, and a failed refresh says so loudly instead of quietly
using stale data.

The other: the bench maths was locally sensible and globally bland, filling
seven slots with whatever scored best in isolation. It now has targets -- a
third QB, two lottery tickets, a first-year back the market has actually
noticed -- applied as pressure that grows as picks run out, so a gap is filled
mid-draft at a fair price instead of forced in the last round at any price,
which is how the earlier MIN_AT_POS attempt produced a -130 quarterback. A
player who already owns a small role is discounted: he is worth having and
worth less than the same points of upside.

Measured over 30 boards, honestly: most of the shape was already there from the
insurance/ticket work, and the quotas add less than expected. QB3 goes from 80%
to 100% of drafts, and "singles hitters" halve, 2 to 1. Tickets were already at
4 and buzzed rookie backs at 93% before any quota existed. Starting lineup is
unchanged at 1864.

A quota also has a value floor, added after the first version bought the LABEL:
a "ticket" behind a starter whose role projects to nothing scores p x 0, and
the bonus alone was enough to roster him.
38221c592026-08-27 23:17

Bench: read the market, because depth charts and ADP lag camp news

The bench valuation leaned on Sleeper's depth_chart_order, which is a roster
formality that says nothing about a position battle. ADP is no better: measured
today, the median player moved 0.6 picks between our locked FFC snapshot and the
live feed, and exactly one of 229 moved more than fifteen. Meanwhile MarShawn
Lloyd took 264,987 Sleeper adds in a single day and his ADP moved 3.8 picks.
Beat reporters and position coaches know things in August that the consensus
prices in September, and trending adds are the fastest free proxy for it.

robo/buzz.py pulls trending adds net of drops over 72h, caches to data/buzz.json,
and refreshes daily ahead of the board rebuild. It is used two ways. The
automated one is deliberately small: a capped lift to P(opportunity), applied
only to a player who is ALREADY a listed backup with a real projection, which
moves 7 of 175 bench slots across 25 boards. The raw feed is noisy enough to put
a kicker, a team defence and an unrostered QB in its top twenty, so it adjusts a
case the depth chart already supports rather than inventing one.

The more valuable half is for a human: `python -m robo.buzz` prints where the
crowd and our board disagree. Every one of the current top 25 is late,
unpriced, or absent from our board entirely. That list is worth reading before
Sunday, because it is the one place the bot can show what it does not know.
207fc2e62026-08-27 22:45

Bench picks: insurance and lottery tickets, not expected points

A bench player scores us nothing in the ordinary case, so ranking bench
candidates by their own expected production answers a question nobody asked.
Raw points drafted a QB3 we would never start; VORP, which replaced it, drafted
no backups at all -- measured over 40 boards the bench carried literally zero
conditional value, because every genuine backup grades terribly on VORP by
construction. Both were optimising the wrong thing.

robo/bench.py values a bench pick the way the roster actually uses one:
P(opportunity) x what the role is worth, less what we would otherwise field.
That splits by whose starter he stands behind -- insurance on ours, a ticket on
someone else's -- with INSURANCE_WEIGHT as the dial between them, plus bye
coverage as a separate schedule term. Depth charts come from Sleeper's
depth_chart_order, ~93% covered on our board.

Over 40 boards: insurance 0.0 -> 11.7 points of cover, lottery 0.0 -> 72.8
points of upside, bye holes 2.0 -> 1.0, starting lineup unchanged at 1861 vs
1864. Reaching went DOWN, -10.6 -> -6.7 picks past market, because bench value
is now multiplied by the chance we actually lose the player: a handcuff going
at ADP 263 is free in five rounds and is no longer taken in round 8.

Three things had to be fixed after they measured badly rather than before.
Without the scarcity term it reached ~170 picks past market and drafted an
undrafted quarterback. Bye coverage at 12 points a hole was not a tiebreaker
but the driver, taking players with no path to a role. Counting bye holes at
positions we had not drafted yet double-charged the DP's own starter needs.

Every rate in bench.py is a stated assumption, not a fitted number. The current
mix runs about 6:1 toward lottery over insurance, which suits a team that is
not the favourite; INSURANCE_WEIGHT moves it.
84e6a74e2026-08-27 22:23

Draft agent: stop calling the highest-scoring QB "bench depth"

choose_pick valued the bench branch with a flat 0.30 discount, which was fine,
and then selected the player with max(pool, key=proj_pts) over a pool spanning
every position, which was not. Under 2QB scoring the eight highest-scoring
players on the board are all quarterbacks, so that was a standing order to
draft one -- Cam Ward, -51 VORP, arriving as "bench depth" ahead of startable
backs. A bench pick fills no lineup slot, so the question it poses is whether
the player beats what we could get free later, and that is VORP. Starter
branches keep ranking on raw points, which is correct rather than merely
conventional: a QB in the superflex slot really does outscore any WR there by
about 110 points.

Measured over 50 boards: mean bench VORP -11.0 -> -9.4, and quarterbacks fall
from 14% of our bench to none. Starting lineups are untouched -- median 1865,
min 1800, max 1905 before and after -- which is the point, since a bench player
does not start.

Two fixes were tried first and are recorded here because both looked obviously
right and measured worse. Scoring the whole DP on VORP drafts only two QBs and
force-picks a -130 backup in round 15, because VORP cannot see that the
superflex slot wants a quarterback. Weighting the bench by START_WEIGHT swapped
Bryce Young for a worse Geno Smith and left the QB share at 14%.

Consequence worth stating: we now roster exactly two quarterbacks in every
simulated draft, so a bye or an injury means starting a waiver QB in a slot
that cannot be left empty. Forcing a third via MIN_AT_POS was measured too and
is worse -- the floor only binds in the last rounds, when the worst pick of the
draft falls from -37.5 to -118.9. If we want QB insurance it has to be bought
mid-draft as a real need, not backstopped at the end.

69fa9f202026-08-27 22:03

Mock draft: give the other 11 teams their own keepers and needs

The mock ran the rest of the league as a single ADP countdown with stdev
jitter -- no team identity, no roster needs, every non-our pick just the next
name off one shared list. That looked indefensible with these keepers, since 10
of the 24 are quarterbacks: two teams have both their starters already while
four, us included, kept none and need two apiece. Redraft ADP prices a league
where all 12 teams need two QBs, which this is not.

--opponents needs gives each rival its own keepers and has it draft to its own
starters before taking bench darts. Measured against the old model over 50
boards, it barely matters: QBs off the board by our round-4 pick is 9 either
way, roster shape is unchanged, and it costs about 15 projected starter points,
under 1%. The two errors cancel. That is worth having established rather than
assumed, and worth keeping runnable for next season when the keeper mix differs.

Both models stay. Neither claims rivals draft well -- the needs model still ends
every team at exactly 3 QBs, because once its starters are set it falls through
to best-available and in 2QB ADP that is usually another quarterback. The
per-team position counts are returned so that stays checkable.
46515d002026-08-27 21:44

Mock drafts now run the policy that will actually be on the clock

There were two pick engines and only the wrong one could be run as a mock.
draft_sim picks by best blend_rank with no notion of roster construction, which
in a 2QB league drafts zero quarterbacks -- fine for the question it was built
for (which slot is worth more, now settled) and badly misleading as a preview.
The policy that actually drafts, choose_pick's DP over our remaining picks, had
no offline path at all. mock_draft.py gives it one, over the frozen keeper
board, with --runs to jitter the rest of the league by each player's published
ADP stdev. Over 50 boards it takes 3 QBs every single time and lands a starting
lineup in a 1813-1915 point band.

Building it surfaced a worse bug than the one it was meant to answer: the policy
was not deterministic. It chose its plan branch by iterating a set of strings,
and Python randomises string hashing per process, so on an exact tie the same
board produced a different pick from one run to the next. That makes any mock
unreproducible and would have had the bot publish reasoning it could not
reproduce. Ties now resolve in a fixed order: a named starter need beats FLEX,
which leftovers can fill later, and both beat bench depth.

Also caches the DP's positional shelf lookup. It is a pure function of position
and pick number within a call, so the cache cannot change behaviour -- verified
identical picks -- and it takes choose_pick from 0.54s to 0.01s, which is what
makes 50-board runs practical.
93e6305a2026-08-27 20:58

Draft board is now the keeper source of truth

The commissioner filled the 2026 board and assigned every team's keepers to
their pick numbers, which makes the board a better source than the roster
"keepers" field it used to read. The field had already drifted: one team still
claimed Jordan Love whose board pick is James Cook, and another was blank with
two keepers on the board. league_keepers now prefers the board, freezes it to
data/keepers_2026.json, and reports the disagreements instead of silently
picking a side -- a keeper the board releases is a real asset back in the pool,
and Love goes straight into the plan at our round-4 pick.

Also fixes a bug that would have wrecked draft day: the agent computed the pick
on the clock as len(picks) + 1. Keeper assignments occupy their pick numbers
before the draft opens, so that ran 24 picks ahead from the start and never
reconverged -- and it is the number submitted with the pick. It now takes the
lowest unfilled pick, and derives the picks we own from the board rather than a
hardcoded list of forfeited rounds.

The slot simulator was burning all 204 picks against a pool already 24 players
short, double-counting every keeper and making the late rounds look barrener
than they will be. Keeper picks now consume no one.
4817f43c2026-08-27 18:26

Fix the bot going silent: GPU loss, dropped messages, dead refresh

Roboner stopped answering the GroupMe for about an hour on 27 Aug. Three
separate faults, none of which showed up as "down" — the process was alive,
the port answered, the heartbeat was green.

Ollama auto-upgraded itself while `ollama serve` was running, so the live
server kept its old binary against swapped-out CUDA libraries, silently lost
the GPU, and reloaded every model onto the CPU. A reply went from ~6s to over
the 240s timeout. Restarting Ollama restored it.

Worse, the missed messages were gone for good: the chat cursor was advanced
when messages were FETCHED, before any reply was attempted, so a failure
mid-reply buried them permanently instead of retrying. The cursor is now
staged on fetch and only committed once the batch is handled, with a
three-strike skip so one bad batch can't wedge the loop.

The daily refresh had also been failing since 25 Aug: its scheduled-task
wrapper redirected into the same refresh.log the pipeline writes itself, and
cmd's >> takes a non-sharing handle, so every run died with PermissionError
on its first log line while still reporting success. Output now goes to
refresh.err.log.

Added RobonerGpu.ps1, asserting that Ollama actually holds the GPU rather
than merely answering. Both the start script and the watchdog now check
placement, and the watchdog checks it on every run — this failure keeps the
heartbeat green, so a liveness check structurally cannot see it. Also pinned
keep_alive per-request, since the machine-wide 30s setting was unloading the
17GB model between every reply.

AccessDenied, including the one that shipped. Now the archived url is never
trusted: every image is re-uploaded from its local copy on first use and only
our own url is cached. Cleared 122 stale entries; spot checks return 200.

- Four pool rows have paths that were never extracted under media/ and exist
  only inside exports/*.zip (searched all 93,255 files under media/ by message
  id and by hash — no copy). Read from the folder first, fall back to the zip,
  so all 176 rows resolve.

  same way and the only symptom is a broken image in the chat.

Capability denial. Asked "do you post gifs now?", it replied "No gifs. I run
on text and cold calculation" — and attached a gif. The question matched none
of CODE_QUESTION_HINTS, so selfdoc never loaded and the model answered from
persona alone and made something up.

- Hints now cover capability phrasings ("do you post", "can you send", "are
  you able", "what can you"). Ordinary chatter still doesn't trigger it.
- Persona rule against misstating what it can do in either direction, naming
  the "just text and cold calculation" move specifically.
- selfdoc's ARCHITECTURE never mentioned media at all, so even a loaded digest
  had no answer. Added media/archive_media/chat_memory/selfdoc, and routed

- That blurb also claimed "~63KB across 15 modules" against an actual 188KB

Verified against the real model: it answers accurately and attaches a working

b9db19272026-08-25 14:27

Reaction images: whitelisted pool from the GroupMe Archive

The bot had exactly one usable image, because media.py requires a
hand-written description per file before anything becomes visible to the
model. The Archive already captions and embeds every synced image nightly,
so borrow that instead of rebuilding it.

- archive_media.py: read-only pull of a WHITELISTED subset of the Archive
  into data/media_pool.db, mirroring how chat_memory.py borrows the league
  group. Two sources, both vetted: every image ever posted in the league
  GroupMe (44), and a keyword cut of 'robot' GIFs (132). Allowlist, not
  blocklist — the same database holds quarantined groups.
- Selection is a query, not a snapshot, so the Archive's nightly captioning
  feeds the pool with no new process. refresh.py gains a media-pool step.
- Hybrid FTS5 + nomic-embedding search with RRF fusion, same shape as
  chat_memory. chat_memory._BLOCK filters captions on the way IN.
- Separate PICK_CUTOFF (0.32) for posting, much tighter than the 0.5 browse
  cutoff: measured on this pool, real matches land at 0.22-0.29 and nonsense
  at 0.37+. Keyword-only hits are rejected outright — FTS ORs its terms, so
  "a giraffe eating spaghetti" was returning a gif of someone eating
  pancakes. Ollama down means no images, which is the correct failure.
- chat_responder: the model now writes [img: a sad cartoon robot] and the
  pool resolves it, instead of receiving a catalog of every image. Bare
  [img:slug] still hits the curated manifest, which stays as the vetted tier.
- Posting reuses the original GroupMe CDN url; m.groupme.com uploads are
  re-hosted to i.groupme.com lazily and cached, same as media.py.

Also fix Start/StopRoboner.bat, which exited nonzero and skipped their final
line. `timeout` was unqualified, so Git Bash's coreutils build shadowed the
Windows one; and Windows timeout.exe itself fails whenever stdin is
redirected, which any Run button or pipe does. Replaced with a fully
qualified ping delay that never reads stdin.
